00The reviewed build was not a results build

Read this before acting on any review.

Stop condition

All three reviews describe numbers that do not exist.

The PDF they read carries its own watermark — “DRAFT — the numbers in this build are NOT from a run on the archive” — and the figures they quote are demo-generator output. This is not a quibble about provenance. It changes what the paper is about.

One reviewer builds a recommendation on “the reimplementation scored higher — 21 patients.” The real finding is the opposite, and it is stronger: the released artefacts under the documented rule call every uninfected patient infected.

Two consequences bind everything below. First, no number enters prose until the re-run produces it — the last real cache no longer exists. Second, the reviewers’ methodological points stand regardless. They were reasoning about the design, and on the design they are largely right — including on one defect this package had already found by simulation, and one it had got conceptually wrong.

Points not adopted, and why

“The AURC figure disagrees with the text.” Not an editing task. In v2 the figures and numbers came from different code paths; in v3 both descend from one Study object and a stamp check refuses a build across a mismatch. The class of defect is removed rather than the instances repaired.

A model card for each checkpoint. Adopted, with one change: sourced from the released artefacts and the source publication, never reconstructed. Where the release does not state something, the cell reads NOT REPORTED. A model card filled in by inference is worse than none.

“The study has approximately 80% power to detect 6%.” Do not write this. It is a pre-hoc power statement computed after the sample size was fixed by someone else’s test split, and it assumes a base sensitivity. §10.4 replaces it with the detectable-effect calculation, which answers the same reader question without assuming the answer.

02The hierarchy of claims

A methodological diagnostic-AI paper demonstrated on malaria microscopy — not a malaria detector paper.

A detector and a patient-level diagnostic system are not the same object. The patient decision is a function of the checkpoint, the post-processing, the aggregation rule, the threshold and the calibration — and patient-level results are routinely reported naming only the first.
#ContributionWhat it is
1A measurement problemPatient-level performance is not determined by detector output alone; a decision layer sits between them and is rarely reported.
2An experimental demonstrationUnder matched post-processing and fixed weights, varying the rule moved performance further than varying the checkpoint. Scope: this cohort, these levels.
3A reproducibility findingThe published patient-level result was not reconstructible from the released artefacts under the documented procedure.
4A design frameworkIdentifiability and design gates that refuse comparisons incapable of producing a non-null result, before unblinding.
5A clinical cautionThis dataset cannot establish deployment-level performance, and the protocol says so before the results rather than after.
R4 is evidence for contribution 1. It is not the paper’s endpoint. No sentence may present it as a recommended rule.

03Three quantities that must never share a name

The commonest way this manuscript misleads is by letting one word do three jobs.

NameSymbolWhat it is
the published result—the accuracy the source publication reports. An external fact. We did not compute it and cannot verify it.
the reconstructed ruleR0our implementation of the published rule as documented, run on the released weights and images
the comparatorR0the same object, in its role as the baseline of every paired test

The confirmatory comparison is therefore R4 against our reconstruction of the published rule, never “against the published system.”

Generated sentence — required

The released artefacts did not reconstruct the source study’s reported patient-level accuracy. We treated the reconstructed rule as the prespecified comparator for all paired analyses, and interpret the discrepancy as a reproducibility finding about the release rather than as evidence about the original implementation.

Vocabulary ban. “Failed reproduction” is retired. It licenses the reading the original paper was wrong, which this study cannot support.

04The estimands, stated before any analysis

The dataset does not merely limit precision. It changes what is being estimated.

4.1 Screening specificity — a cohort quantity, not a property of a rule. All 54 uninfected patients come from Montpellier, which also supplied training material. Specificity is perfectly collinear with centre and with internal/external domain. This is rank deficiency, not low power: no estimator separates them and no sample size fixes it.

Estimand. The probability that the rule calls a patient uninfected, among uninfected patients from the Montpellier cohort of this archive, under the reconstructed RT-DETR pipeline.

Forbidden: “the rule controls false positives.” Required: “the rule reduced false-positive calls in the Montpellier-negative subset of this archive.”

4.2 Screening sensitivity — conditioned on enriched fields. Every field from an infected patient contains at least one parasite by the archive’s selection protocol. Accumulation and counting rules are being evaluated under sampling far more favourable than screening.

Estimand. The probability that the rule calls a patient infected, among infected patients, when every examined field is known to contain a parasite.

That clause is not a limitation to be confessed later. It is part of the estimand — and §13 measures how far the answer moves when it is relaxed.

4.3 Accuracy — demoted. At 68% prevalence with a non-representative species mix, accuracy is a design-dependent summary. It stays in the factorial because it is the one quantity defined for every cell, and it is never a headline.

4.4 Speciation — a fixed denominator. Evaluated on the truly infected patients regardless of the screening call, so the denominator is identical across rules and the pairing survives.

08Gates

Order is fixed. Each gate is evaluated before anything it could contaminate.

identifiability → yield → pre-declared checkpoint → factorial
    → reconstruction → design → confirm
§8.3 — changed

The checkpoint is now pre-declared

v2 selected the confirmatory checkpoint by maximum baseline headroom. An independent reviewer and this package’s own null simulation found the same defect from opposite directions:

headroom = b + (patients both rules get wrong)

where b is the numerator of the test statistic. Selecting the largest headroom selects, in part, the largest b. Simulated at three comparable arms the size rises to ≈6.5% against a nominal 5% — an inflation, not the promised conservatism. The argument v2 gave for it (“headroom reads the baseline only”) is simply wrong.

v3 pre-declares RT-DETR, named in the registration, chosen because it is the architecture the source study reports — a fact independent of any outcome in this dataset. Headroom is retained as a descriptive feasibility statement, never as a selector.

Two analyses remain, both pre-specified. Max-discordance selection is reported as a sensitivity analysis: it is level-preserving by construction, since the exact McNemar conditions on n = b+c and conditional on n the statistic is Binomial(n, ½) whatever else is true. And the size simulation itself goes in the supplement — a design paper that found a size inflation in its own procedure should show it.

Identifiability fails on this archive and qualifies rather than stops. Yield is post hoc and declared so; a refused arm is named with its cause, which requires the YOLOv5x probe rather than silence. Reconstruction refusal is the expected outcome and is a finding. Design refuses a comparison whose candidate is screening-equivalent to the comparator, with discordance taken among the uninfected — and is applied to the sensitivity arm as well as the primary.

09Factorial attribution — rebuilt

The paper’s second contribution, and v2’s weakest analysis.

v2 compared a range across fourteen rules with a range across two or three checkpoints. A range grows with the number of levels; the two factors have wildly different level counts; the comparison is outlier-sensitive. v2 also claimed the interaction “is arithmetically the residual and is not separately estimable.”

Retraction

That claim is wrong.

Every patient contributes an outcome under every rule–checkpoint cell. The design is a complete factorial with 170 repeated patient-level observations per cell, not one observation per cell. What v2 actually lacked was independent residual error at the cell-mean level — a different and much narrower statement.

The v3 primary analysis is a patient-clustered model on the binary patient-level outcome:

logit P(Y_irc = 1) = μ + α_r + β_c + (αβ)_rc + u_i

Fitted as a GEE with an exchangeable working correlation and robust standard errors clustered on patient. Reported: the marginal rule effect, the marginal checkpoint effect, the interaction with a test, and the contrast between the two marginals — which is the paper’s claim.

Ranges become descriptive, secondary, and labelled. The permutation null for the checkpoint factor is retained because it is valid. No permutation null is reported for the rule factor — with one observation per cell-mean there is no permutation that removes a rule effect without removing the values it is made of, and v2’s attempt returned a null equal to the observed spread to the last decimal.

Required wording

Forbidden: “the rule matters more than the detector”, and any multiplier computed from a ratio of ranges.

Within the levels surviving the yield gate and under matched post-processing, the marginal effect of the aggregation rule on patient-level accuracy exceeded the marginal effect of the detector checkpoint by [Δ] on the log-odds scale (95% CI [·], patient-clustered). The descriptive accuracy range across rules was [·] percentage points against [·] across checkpoints; these are ranges over factors with different numbers of levels and are not variance components.

10The confirmatory comparison

Intersection–union co-primary on the pre-declared checkpoint. One candidate, one comparator.

Specificity by superiority (one-sided exact McNemar); sensitivity by non-inferiority at a 5 percentage-point margin. Both components one-sided in the same direction at α = 0.05. An IU test needs no multiplicity correction — and inherits the worst component’s level, which is why the method below matters.

10.2 Discordances first. The inferential object is the paired 2×2. Report it as such, before any percentage-point difference — a reader currently cannot reconstruct the result from the manuscript.

§10.3 — method fixed

The non-inferiority bound is Tango’s score interval

The percentile bootstrap v2 used is only first-order accurate, and at this endpoint’s parameters — a comparator sensitivity near 0.97, so per-patient differences almost always 0 or −1 — it does not hold its level. Simulated type I error at the NI null was 0.063 to 0.076 against a nominal 0.05, and because this is half of a co-primary the whole endpoint inherited it.

Tango holds (0.017–0.044 simulated) and retains 0.93 power. Both limits are computed and reported; Tango governs.

10.4 Detectable effect, not observed power. Observed power is a monotone function of the p-value and is not reported. Reported instead: given the realised sample size and discordance, the smallest true effect the procedure would find with 80% probability — including the fact that below five discordant pairs no split whatsoever reaches a one-sided 0.05. That is a property of the design, not of the detector.

10.5 The verdict is one generated sentence, each clause reading its own component, reused verbatim in abstract, results, discussion and conclusion. If specificity passes and sensitivity does not: the co-primary endpoint was not met, and no superiority claim is made. The design working as intended is a result, not a disappointment.

11Calibration — split

v2 mixed binary screening calibration with six-class calibration in one set of numbers.

AnalysisQuantityReported
A — InfectionP(infected) per patientBrier, ECE against its finite-sample null, calibration slope and intercept, ICI, reliability diagram
B — Speciationthe class posterior among truly infected patients onlymulticlass Brier stated by formula, per-class reliability, explicit statement of whether probabilities sum to one
Reported separately. Never pooled.
  • Four decimal places. A Brier reported as 0.000 is a formatting failure all three reviewers flagged, and it is indistinguishable from a genuine zero — which would be impossible here.
  • Every metric cross-fitted out of fold, with the fold count and the number of degenerate folds beside it.
  • Reliability diagrams carry bin counts, intervals and a score histogram. A connected line over sparse bins is not a reliability diagram.
  • Calibration acts on the screening statistic only — a monotone map on one number — so it can move the infection boundary and cannot reorder species. No speciation result may be attributed to it.
  • ECE is not an inferential test. Permitted: “the observed ECE lies within the finite-sample variability expected under the stated null.” Forbidden: any wording implying calibration was established.

13Field-sampling stress test

New. The highest-value addition in all three reviews — worth more than any additional rule.

Every field from an infected patient contains a parasite. Accumulation rules are therefore evaluated under an assumption that screening violates. The concern is not that all performance is inflated. It is that the ranking of the rules may be an artefact of the enrichment, since R0 accumulates evidence without bound and R4 requires a count.

Design. Hold the detector cache fixed. For each infected patient, construct smears at a declared positive-field rate π ∈ {1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1} by combining that patient’s enriched fields with parasite-free fields drawn from uninfected patients of the same centre. Re-run every rule at every π.

Reported: each rule’s screening sensitivity and specificity against π — and, the actual question, whether the ordering of the rules is stable across π.

Declared in advance

This is a sensitivity analysis under a stated resampling model, not an estimate of screening performance. The negative fields come from a different patient, so it tests the rules’ response to evidence dilution rather than the full screening problem. If the ordering inverts at low π, that outranks the confirmatory comparison — and saying so here, before the answer is known, is what makes it reportable either way.

21What this protocol refuses to claim

Written before the analysis, so it cannot be softened afterwards.

Out of scope by construction

Not caveats. Statements the design cannot support, whatever the numbers turn out to be.

  • that the source publication is wrong
  • that R4 is a recommended rule
  • that any specificity estimate is site-invariant
  • that any performance figure transports to screening
  • that calibration was established
  • that the rule factor dominates the detector as a general fact
  • that anything here bears on clinical utility or deployment readiness
Patient-level decision rules are consequential, this dataset is insufficient to support a deployment recommendation, and a registered design can expose that limitation rather than conceal it.

22Acquisition — how the archive becomes evidence

Where the detections come from. Two of these choices change what every downstream number means, which is why they are protocol rather than appendix.

22.1 The cohort is the manifest’s, not the filesystem’s. One smear is one patient. Centre and reference diagnosis come from the manifest, mapped through tables written out rather than derived. A species or centre value the mapping does not contain is refused; a smear carrying two of either is refused; an image the manifest lists but the archive does not hold makes ingest exit non-zero. Each alternative is a silent change of denominator that nothing downstream could detect.

22.2 The transformation is measured, not inferred. The archive ships test and test-zoom as two label trees over the same smears, so the source study’s zoom correction is a count. mag ingest counts it and writes it beside the patient table; the analysis quotes it from there, so a reviewer re-running from a published cache never needs the 6 GB archive. Where it was never counted, every macro says NOT MEASURED rather than falling back to a plausible fraction.

22.3 The cache is pre-suppression, at a declared floor. Detection runs at 0.001 with suppression effectively disabled, and the manifest records nms="none". Every rule’s threshold is applied downstream, so a cache written at the published 0.25 operating point could not evaluate a rule below it and the three post-processing conditions of §6 would collapse into one.

22.4 The class map comes from the checkpoint, or is declared. Names embedded in the checkpoint, matched through a synonym table that tolerates case, spacing and the usual abbreviations and nothing else. Any unmatched name is a refusal. Falling back to classes.txt order must be requested explicitly and declared in the manuscript: a wrong order relabels every detection with no other symptom.

§22.5 — upstream of the yield gate

A silent detector is a bug, not a result.

A run producing fewer boxes than fields, or boxes in under half the fields, marks its own cache suspect, and every analysis stage then refuses that arm. The yield gate protects the analysis; this protects the interpretation.

A checkpoint that returns two boxes from four and a half thousand fields has not been loaded or driven correctly. Reporting it as a weak detector would be a claim about the checkpoint that the evidence does not support.

22.6 Reproducibility of the acquisition. Detection is resumable at smear granularity, and a shard is reused only when its fingerprint matches exactly: the image list, the confidence floor, the image size, the class map, and the checkpoint’s SHA-256. The cache carries, and every downstream stage verifies, that SHA in two places, a content hash pinned to an exact serialisation, and the per-smear shards themselves — which must cover at least half the cohort before any result may be written. A table assembled by any other means has none of those and cannot acquire them.

→Implementation

Built. Every section of this protocol is implemented and held in place by tests.

Status

Every section implemented; 292 tests, three Python versions, two platforms.

The substrate was kept rather than rewritten. Its regression tests pin defects found by four adversarial audits, each confirmed by running it: suppression keyed on image_id alone removed 28% of detections under matched and 38% under source; the non-inferiority bound did not hold its level; the co-primary verdict printed backwards whenever specificity was the failing component. A rewrite would have discarded the tests that catch them.

The rebuild then found five more in itself, each now pinned by a test named after it: a Kendall τ that added tied pairs where τ-b subtracts them, returning 0.67 for two identical rankings and turning a stable ordering into an unstable one; a cross-artefact check silently disconnected by a rename, reporting success while comparing nothing; a manifest that hashed memory rather than disk; a package version drifted to 1.0.0 while the metadata said 3.0.0; and complete separation making two implementations of the attribution model disagree by 4×108.

What remains

The run. python run_local.py --root <archive> on the machine with the GPU: ingest takes seconds, detection takes hours and is resumable at smear granularity, analysis takes four minutes. Expect the reconstruction gate to refuse — that refusal is the paper’s central finding, and it belongs in the text before it belongs in a flag.

Then the manuscript, in the order §20 sets out, with every number read from a macro and never typed. mag audit refuses the build if a macro is missing, a verdict is typed where a generated sentence exists, or a figure came from a different run — and there is no bypass flag.

Ten declarations are yours, and nothing here will guess them: the author list and CRediT statement, both ethics approvals, consent for publication, funding, the AI-use declaration, the repository URL, the registration identifier, the code archive DOI, and the data availability statement. The audit checks that each is present and non-empty; it does not supply them, because a pipeline that filled them in would be inventing an ethics approval.

On revisiting the manuscript

It cannot be rewritten yet.

Three reviewers read a build whose numbers were placeholders and produced pages of analysis about results that do not exist — including a recommendation built on the reconstruction discrepancy running in the opposite direction to the real one. Writing prose now, against the same absent numbers, reproduces that situation with better wording.

What can be written now is everything that does not depend on a value: the section architecture, the contribution hierarchy, the estimands and their scopes (properties of the dataset, not of any result), the language rules, the claim–evidence–scope skeleton with verdict cells empty, and the Methods in full. That is a substantial share of the paper, and it is the share that is safe to write.
